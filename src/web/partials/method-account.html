Every figure here is fitted from your own upload, by the same code that fits the public example on the Price Sensitivity Lab front page. Sales are rolled up to one row per product per week, and each product is compared against its own history — so the answer reflects what happened when that product's price moved, not the fact that expensive things sell in smaller numbers.

The three things this can't tell you

  • It's a pattern, not a promise. Your prices weren't set at random. They moved for reasons — promotions, seasons, clearance — and those reasons moved sales too. Read this as evidence, not a forecast.
  • Revenue is not profit. A discount that grows revenue can still shrink what you keep. Add your unit cost in the simulator to see both.
  • Your past, not your future. The estimate describes the customers, products and prices in the file you uploaded. If your range, market or competitors have changed since, the numbers may have too — which is what the other markets comparison is for.
